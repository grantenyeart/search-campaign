01 · The basics

What a Search Campaign is

Somebody types what they need into Google. We put the business in front of them, and the click lands on a page built to do one thing: book the job.

●
We build the landing page
Their pricing, their work, and a booking path. Not their homepage.
●
We pick the services, the keywords and the towns
And we police the junk searches daily so they stop paying for clicks that were never going to book.
●
They pay Google directly for the clicks
Their card, their account. We charge a flat management fee on top.
Say it this simply

"We put you in front of people already typing what you do into Google, and we send them to a page built to book you."

04 · How Google decides

Google grades three things

1
How often people click the ad
2
How well the ad matches what they searched
3
What the page does once they land on it
The line

We work all three. Google grades each one against the competition.

That is why two businesses can spend the same money and get completely different results. Score well on all three and Google charges you less for the same position.

06b · What counts

What a conversion is

Define it once, out loud, the first time you say the word. If you do not, they hear a number and expect that many jobs.

●
A call
●
A text
●
A quote request
●
A Book Now click
Booked jobs are separate

Those get tracked in their CRM, not in Google. A conversion is somebody reaching out. It is not a job on the calendar, and an owner who confuses the two will be disappointed on the first monthly report no matter how well the campaign ran.

08 · The math

Say this before you say the number

A conversion is someone who called, texted, requested a quote or hit Book Now. Never turn it into a jobs promise. Do not say "and if half of them book."

Clicks depend on the market

Kansas City detailing runs about $2 a click. Most detailing markets are $3 to $5. Window cleaning is nearer $10. Same $50 a day buys a very different number of clicks, so say "in a market like Kansas City" when you use these.

And $50 a day is a cap, not a bill. In a small market they will not spend all of it, because there are not enough searches.

11 · Objections

You're month to month. We track every call and every lead, we review the numbers at 30, 60 and 90 days, and if it isn't working we change the plan or tell you straight.

13 · The guarantee

Spoken only. Never shown.

Read this before anything else

This never goes on a slide and never goes in the pitch. It is not a feature. It is what you reach for when a prospect has said the risk out loud and nothing else has moved them.

Someone who is ready to sign does not get it.

Word for word
Your campaign books back what you spend with us, ads plus our fee, within your first 60 days live. If it doesn't, you don't pay our fee again until it does.
When

Only after they name the risk themselves: "what if it doesn't work", "I got burned before", "I can't afford to lose this."

Answer with month to month first. Only if they are still stuck does this come out. If they will not pay a flat fee at all, that is the 15% revenue share, and the guarantee never goes with revenue share.

13b · Their number

Work out the number before you offer it

Two months of our fee, plus 60 days of ad spend, divided by their average job price, rounded up.

PlanFee x 260 days adsTheir number
$1,000$2,000$3,00020 jobs
$750$1,500$3,00018 jobs
$500$1,000$3,00016 jobs

At $50 a day and a $250 average job. For commercial work, a signed contract counts as a job.

Where this goes wrong

Take the average job price from their price list or their booking page, not their guess. Owners round up when they are talking about themselves.

Only offer it when their market can clearly hit the number. Our projection has a low case. If the low case does not cover their number, there is no guarantee. That is how we avoid another True Clean.

If we have not run their market before the call, do not promise it on the call. Say you will check their market and confirm in writing today, then send Ryan their business, city, services, average job price and budget.
